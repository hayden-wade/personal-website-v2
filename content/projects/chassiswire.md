# ChassisWire

A better way to document, understand and modify automotive electrical systems.

## Why I started it

Working on an old car can mean jumping between scanned wiring diagrams, connector drawings, forum posts and notes from someone else’s engine swap. The information is there, but the relationships are often hard to follow — and once you modify the car, the factory drawing stops telling the whole story.

I want to bring those relationships into one place: from a vehicle and harness down to an individual connector, pin, wire or splice.

## The idea

ChassisWire is an automotive electrical documentation project. The aim is to make it possible to explore a system at different levels, follow a connection, understand where a component lives and produce a clean diagram you can take into the workshop.

Factory wiring and modified wiring should be distinguishable. A change should have a reason and a history. A community-sourced diagram should carry its source and verification status, so useful information does not lose its provenance when it is shared.

## Built around a real problem

My E30 projects provide the practical test cases: planning an M54B30 swap, working through power-window wiring, and making sense of the connectors and circuits that sit between systems.

The project brings together a few things I already spend time on: electrical engineering, software, old BMWs and the urge to document things properly.

## Where it stands

ChassisWire is in development. I’m working through the product design, domain model and interface before treating it as a finished tool. The initial direction is open source, with the core model centred on vehicles, systems, harnesses, connectors, pins, wires, nets and modifications.

The schematic on the homepage illustrates that direction; it is a concept diagram, not a screenshot of a released application.
