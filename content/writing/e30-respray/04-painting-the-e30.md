# Painting the E30

## The part I did not want to learn on this car

I was happy to learn disassembly, sanding, filler, basic rust repair and welding on the E30. Spraying the final exterior finish was where I drew the line.

I did not have a booth, enough air supply, proper extraction or a controlled environment at home. After putting so many hours into getting the panels straight, trying to spray the entire car in an exposed West Croydon courtyard would have been a spectacular way to ruin months of work.

The Marketplace painter I found charged around $2,000 for his labour. I supplied the paint system myself – from memory, Valspar products on his recommendation – and I remember the paint materials being roughly another $1,200. Exact totals are difficult to reconstruct because some of the project was paid in cash and some small purchases disappeared into normal hardware-store spending, but my all-in memory of the respray project is around the $5,000 mark.

## Wet-on-wet, colour and clear

After the primer had been sanded and the remaining issues corrected, the painter moved into the final paint stages. From memory, a wet-on-wet surfacer/sealer stage went down before colour, followed by the Glacier Blue basecoat and then clear coat.

This was the moment where the entire project changed visually. In the photos immediately after paint, the colour sometimes reads almost grey because of the lighting. Once the car came outside into natural light, the blue appeared properly and it looked completely different.

After months of filler dust, exposed primer and half-stripped panels, seeing the E30 glossy and blue again was genuinely exciting. The clear had that fresh, glassy look that makes even an unfinished car look expensive.

![Glacier Blue returning to the shell in the spray booth.](/images/e30-respray/paint-side.jpg)

## Not a concours paint job

I was very happy with the transformation, but I do not want to rewrite history and pretend the paint job was perfect.

The painter laid the clear on fairly heavily. That gave me plenty of material to work with later, but there was noticeable orange peel. There were also some runs/drips around moulding recesses and a visible build-up around areas such as the front badge opening. It was a budget paint job being done on a car I had prepped myself, not a $20,000 concours restoration.

At the time, I was probably too relieved and excited to care much. I had spent so many nights and weekends sanding that seeing a complete blue car felt like the finish line even though, technically, there was still a lot of work to do.

With more experience, I would have built an extra refining stage into the plan: high-build primer, careful block sanding, guide coat, final inspection under harsh lighting, then paint. And if budget allowed, I would choose a painter based not only on price but on examples of how flat and clean their final clear finishes were.

That said, the core result has proven durable. Years later, the filler has not cracked across the hail-damaged panels and the paint system has held up well, even with the car spending more time outside than I would ideally like.

## The black trim pieces

One thing that made a surprisingly big difference was refinishing the black exterior parts while the car was apart. The painter sprayed the black window-sill/surround pieces and plastic trim, so when they went back onto the fresh Glacier Blue body they looked much sharper than thirty-year-old faded pieces would have.

This is the kind of detail that is easy to skip when budgeting a respray, but old trim beside fresh paint can make a newly painted car still look tired. Doing the black pieces at the same time helped the car look more coherent once reassembled.

![Masking the body while the window surrounds are refinished.](/images/e30-respray/trim-masking.jpg)

## Taking it home

Once the paint had cured enough to handle, I paid the painter and brought the car home. I was on a deadline to move interstate, so there was no leisurely period of admiring the shell. I had boxes of labelled E30 parts waiting for me and a car with no windscreens, mouldings, bumpers or exterior trim.

The next job was to turn a freshly painted shell back into an actual BMW.
