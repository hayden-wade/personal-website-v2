# Putting the E30 back together

## Why labelled bags suddenly mattered

Months earlier, while disassembling the car, putting clips and screws into labelled bags had felt slightly obsessive. During reassembly it felt like one of the smartest things I had done.

I was trying to put the car back together quickly before moving from Adelaide to Sydney. I had tubs of parts, new clips and mounting hardware from BMW, freshly painted panels I was terrified of scratching, and a long list of small jobs where one missing plastic fastener could stop progress.

I had bought a selection of new genuine clips and attachment pieces for the side mouldings and other trim. They were not especially expensive and, after dealing with brittle thirty-year-old originals, it made sense to replace them rather than force tired clips back into fresh paint.

## Windscreens – and the seal that did not fit

The front windscreen became one of the more frustrating parts of the rebuild.

I bought an aftermarket windscreen seal from Peninsula BMW, expecting it to be a straightforward replacement. It was not. I found it too small and could not get it to sit properly. By the time I had wrestled with it, I had marked/damaged it enough that returning it became its own argument, which was particularly irritating because I did not think the fit was right in the first place.

I eventually got the front glass installed using the setup that worked and paid someone to install the lock strip. In hindsight I probably could have bought the lock-strip tool and done that myself, but at that stage I was happy to pay someone to finish a job quickly and correctly.

The rear windscreen was easier. I installed that myself along with its locking strip. I also fitted replacement corner/bottom trim pieces where needed so the glass areas looked properly finished.

## Trim, mouldings and bumpers

From there, the E30 gradually started looking like itself again.

I refitted the long exterior chrome, the door/window chrome strips, side mouldings, door handles, antenna, front grilles, brake/air-duct pieces, badges and the freshly painted bumper assemblies. The front and rear bumpers went back on with their black plastic trims installed.

I fitted new BMW roundel badges, which was a small thing but looked great against fresh paint. Old badges and faded trim can stand out badly after a respray; replacing a few inexpensive finishing pieces made the whole job look more intentional.

## Aftermarket lip and side skirts

The Mr Bodykits front lip and side skirts came back into the picture at this stage. I had already test-fitted them before paint, which I was very glad I did – drilling or discovering fitment problems after fresh paint would have been much more stressful.

The front lip was attached through the lower valance with drilled mounting points and simple hardware/zip-tie-style retention where appropriate. The side skirts used screws plus Sikaflex to pull them against the sill/body.

They looked good once installed, but I was never completely happy with how naturally they followed the car. The plastic always seemed to want to spring away slightly, so the adhesive was constantly working against that tension.

Today I would probably hunt for OEM pieces. At the time, though, I was trying to finish the car on a sensible budget and the aftermarket kit achieved the look I was chasing.

## The first time it looked complete again

There is a particular point in reassembly where a project stops looking like a shell and becomes a car again. For this E30, it was somewhere around the moment the glass, bumpers, side mouldings and front grille were all back in place.

The Glacier Blue paint in daylight looked much more blue than it had in the workshop photos. The black trim gave it contrast, the chrome went back around the windows, and suddenly I could see why I had kept the car rather than selling it as a hail-damaged write-off.

There were still finishing jobs to do. The clear had orange peel and needed proper correction. A few trim details were not perfect. But the hard part – taking a dented old sedan, stripping it, straightening it and getting it painted – was finally behind me.
